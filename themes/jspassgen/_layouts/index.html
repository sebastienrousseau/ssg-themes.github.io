Standard random passwords draw uniformly from character alphabets (uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols) to maximize entropy density per character. EFF Diceware passphrases draw random words from the curated 7,776-word dictionary compiled by the Electronic Frontier Foundation. A 5-word Diceware passphrase provides approximately 65 bits of entropy and is exceptionally easy for humans to type and remember, while remaining immune to brute-force dictionary attacks.

Invented by cryptographic researchers Ari Juels and Ronald L. Rivest, the Honeywords defense stores k - 1 decoy passwords alongside the genuine password in a hashed credential store. To an external attacker who dumps the database and cracks the hashes, all k passwords appear equally valid. When the attacker attempts to authenticate with any decoy password, a canary trap immediately alerts security operations and freezes the compromised account before the attacker can test the real password.

Naive password meters simply count length or reward predictable patterns like adding an exclamation mark at the end. Claude Shannon's information entropy formula H(X) = -∑ p(x) log₂(p(x)) measures the exact uncertainty and unpredictability of the symbol probability distribution. This reveals whether a password has true cryptographic variance or repetitive bias.
