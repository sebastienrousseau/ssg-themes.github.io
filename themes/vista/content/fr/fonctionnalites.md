## An display for every task

Two panels the size of a postage stamp carry more pixels each than a 4K
television. The practical consequence is not the number: it is that text
renders as text at arm's length, so a document is readable rather than
merely present.

## Input without hardware

Look at a control and pinch. There is no wand to lose and no glove to
charge, and the gesture is small enough to use with your hands in your lap
rather than held in front of you.

## Sound that stays put

Audio is placed in the room rather than in your ears. A window you moved to
the left still sounds as though it is to your left, which is what stops a
long session becoming tiring.

## Environments on demand

Replace the room with a landscape when you need to concentrate, and dial it
back when somebody walks in. The dial is continuous rather than a switch.

## Privacy at the sensor

Where you looked is computed on the device and never handed to an
application. An app learns that you pressed a control, not that you
considered three others first.

## Passthrough that convinces

Cameras rebuild the room with low enough latency that reaching for a cup is
not a calculation. Below about twenty milliseconds the hand stops
hesitating.
